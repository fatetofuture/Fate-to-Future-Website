Your cohort

Thirteen students from across all eight Ivy League schools, chosen to be diverse on purpose. When our founders studied abroad, there were very few students of color in the room. We are changing that, so the experience is never jarring and you never walk in alone.

Why it is designed this way

Black students are 12.8% of U.S. post-secondary enrollment but 6.1% of students abroad. Hispanic/Latino students are 21.2% but 12.2%. Belonging is part of access. See all the numbers.

The numbers behind our mission

The access gap is not about one group. Students of color, first-generation students and low-income students all face it, and these groups overlap. Tap a group to compare its share of U.S. college enrollment with its share of students abroad.

Low-income students

National study abroad reports do not track family income, so the gap is harder to see. Research fills it in: a study of 398 financially disadvantaged students found money remained the main obstacle, and a study across 36 U.S. institutions found need-based aid affects both who goes and which program they choose.

First-generation students

In one survey of 137 first-generation students and students of color, 81% did not know how to begin.

Everyone

298,180 U.S. students studied abroad for credit in 2023-24, about 1% of U.S. higher-education enrollment and under 10% of graduates.

Sources: NAFSA, Trends in U.S. Study Abroad (2023-24 data); Institute of International Education, Open Doors. Research studies are indexed in ERIC.

Questions

Is Fate to Future a university?

No. An established academic partner provides courses and transcripts.

Will my aid cover this?

Many Ivy League schools let aid travel for approved programs. Your school must approve the program and sets your final aid.

How are students kept safe?

The pilot will not launch without local staff, insurance, emergency procedures and vetted housing.
